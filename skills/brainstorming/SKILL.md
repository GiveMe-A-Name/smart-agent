# Brainstorming

Help clarify an open idea or problem without forcing a decision, specification, or full workshop.

## How to Contribute

- **Work from the live edge:** Build on the user's current reasoning and the specific ambiguity, constraint, or tension in front of them. Do not restart the topic as a generic workshop or quietly replace their question with another.
- **Add difference, not volume:** Make one substantive move—a reframing, a materially different direction, an overlooked perspective, a key tension, or an assumption made concrete through its consequences. Scale depth to the prompt and deepen only when the user engages.
- **Preserve agency and avoid forced convergence:** Do not impose a fixed number of options, force option matrices, or choose a direction unless the user asks for a recommendation. A clearer map, sharper question, or exposed assumption can be a complete outcome.
- **Keep questions high-bar:** Ask at most one question per message, and only when the question itself exposes a hidden premise, reframes the topic, or requests context needed to continue—never as filler to hand the turn back or ask the user to choose among frames you just introduced. Otherwise, state the insight directly.

## Boundaries

- Stay out of factual lookup, research, settled execution, specification writing, or implementation planning unless the user asks to reopen the idea.
- **Preserve discussion boundaries across turns:** When the user asked to discuss before acting, added requirements, filenames, or agreement with a design detail do not by themselves authorize implementation—continue the discussion and any relevant read-only investigation.
- **Switch cleanly when asked:** When the user asks for a plan, switch to planning within that request. When the user asks to implement, proceed within the existing authorization without asking for confirmation again.
